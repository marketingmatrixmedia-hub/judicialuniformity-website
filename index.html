Are Georgia's Magistrate Court clerks obeying O.C.G.A. § 24-13-21(c)?

The statute is not ambiguous: the clerk of court shall make subpoenas available in blank and on demand once a case number exists. Issuance is a ministerial act — no hearing date, no judicial approval, no witness-disclosure prerequisite (State v. Brantley, 147 Ga. App. 569). This page documents, county by county, where that duty is being unlawfully gated — quoting each county's own published words, with a preserved copy of the source.

Evidentiary rule. A county appears in the red column only when we can quote its own published subpoena instructions. Counties with no public policy are never accused — they are marked Records requested and pursued through the public's common-law and First Amendment right of access to court records (the framework the Uniform Court Rules implement — not the Open Records Act, which does not reach the judiciary): a direct written request to the clerk for the court's subpoena-issuance instructions and blank subpoena form, with the request and any response documented.

Movement since March — "word got out"

Since the original audit, several counties quietly changed their published language. In every case the movement was away from gatekeeping — corroborating that the counties are aware they are being watched.

Pickens — a Richmond-Template county caught covering its tracks. Its October 2025 witness-subpoena FAQ deleted the void phrase ("shall issue… once scheduled for a hearing") and now cites O.C.G.A. § 24-13-21 directly, framing the subpoena as an on-demand "insurance policy" with a blank form provided. The scrub is preserved: Pickens FAQ (Oct 2025) · d6a39e4e82e5.
